How this works

Risk comes from a model trained on 264,434 street crimes recorded by the Chicago Police Department in 2015–2024: robbery, battery, assault, sexual assault, homicide, kidnapping, pickpocketing and purse snatching on streets, sidewalks, alleys, parks, parking lots and transit, excluding domestic incidents.

The city is split into 250 m cells and the week into 12 time slots (weekday or weekend × six 4-hour windows). The map shows the forecast for the week after the data ends. Each street takes the risk of the cells it passes through.

The safer route minimises distance × (1 + level × risk). In a test on 250 random walks, routes chosen with a risk map built only from 2015–2023 data, at the Balanced setting, were about 4% longer and passed 44–51% fewer real 2024 crimes than the shortest routes.

Limits: this is reported crime, not all crime; the city shifts crime locations within the block for privacy; a lower-risk route is not a guarantee of safety.
